PURPOSE AND INTERFACE
Purpose: binary semantic segmentation of the lumen in ureteroscopy images.
This is an initial research component, not the final digital twin. It does not
predict irrigation flow or intrarenal pressure and is not clinically validated.
Input: an RGB ureteroscopy image, resized to 256x256 with bilinear interpolation
and normalized to float32 [0,1]; PyTorch input shape is N x 3 x 256 x 256.
Output: a 256x256 predicted lumen mask. Apply sigmoid to model logits, then
threshold probabilities at >=0.5: lumen=1, background=0. No postprocessing.
Architecture: small U-Net, 121,177 parameters, encoder channels 8/16/32 with a
64-channel bottleneck, skip connections, and group normalization.

TRAINING DATA AND SETTINGS
Training: 311 image-mask pairs from video_3, Patient 2.
Validation: 99 pairs from both video_7 parts, Patient 4.
Test: 966 pairs: p_003=505, p001=100, p006=361.
Patient 2/4 mappings use the publication Table I information supplied by the
user. p_003 is associated with Patient 3 through exact-image matches to the
publication-mapped Videos 4, 5, and 6.
Uses the existing processed split manifest and binary masks. Seed 42,
8 epochs, batch size 4, Adam learning rate 0.001, and equal-weight BCE plus
soft Dice loss. Trained on an NVIDIA RTX 3050 Laptop GPU.

TRAINING AUGMENTATION ONLY
- Random rotations between -7.5 and +7.5 degrees, applied identically to image
  and mask. Bilinear interpolation for images; nearest-neighbor for masks.
  Fixed canvas and zero fill; masks remain binary.
- Brightness and contrast factors sampled independently from 0.9 to 1.1.
- A 20% chance of mild Gaussian blur with radius 0.3-0.7 pixels, image only.
Validation and test images receive no augmentation.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS AND OBSERVED ERRORS
- p001 and p006 are filename-derived groups whose publication-level patient/
  case identities remain unverified. Complete patient independence cannot be
  confirmed, although known source overlap and exact duplicates were removed.
- Training and validation each represent only one known patient. Nearby video
  frames are correlated; results do not establish broad patient generalization.
- Small and partially obscured lumen regions may be missed, fragmented,
  enlarged, or poorly localized. For 52 test images with annotated lumen area
  <=2% of the processed image, Version 2 mean Dice was only 0.2373; 5 had zero
  overlap. This area cutoff is descriptive, not a clinical size measurement.
- Border false positives, blur/lighting sensitivity, and annotation ambiguity
  remain. Augmentation helped many frames but did not consistently solve these
  problems. Ground-truth binarization assumes bright mask pixels denote lumen;
  resizing can alter aspect ratio and does not repair original annotations.
- Version 2 improved test metrics over Baseline, but its validation metrics were
  slightly lower. The test set was repeatedly examined during development and
  informed version selection; these are exploratory results, not a fresh final
  evaluation. Independent patient-verified validation is still needed.

WHY VERSION 3 WAS NOT SELECTED
Version 3 tested Tversky loss with alpha=0.4 for false positives and beta=0.6
for false negatives, keeping the other Version 2 settings fixed. It improved
validation Dice to 0.5599, but reduced test Dice to 0.4174 and test IoU to
0.3047. Test foreground recall fell from Version 2's 56.78% to 37.08%, and
zero-overlap test images increased from 21 to 77. It therefore did not improve
test-set lumen detection consistency and was not selected as the current model.
